When your agent wants to run something it doesn't have standing permission for,
it stops and asks. Normally you'd answer that at your Mac. With Nomo you can
answer it from your phone, and the agent picks up immediately.

<Warning>
  **Turn it on first.** Remote approvals are **off by default**. Settings →
  Sessions → **Remote approvals** → **Answer on iPhone**. Until that switch is
  on, prompts stay in your terminal and your phone only gets told about them.
</Warning>

## Two places to answer

The island **announces**. The app **answers**. They offer different things on
purpose.

## Everything you can answer

| Option | Where | What the agent gets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Allow** | Island, app | Runs the action and continues. |
| **Deny** | Island, app | Skips it and continues without it. |
| **Always allow Bash (this session)** | App only | Allows it and stops asking **for the rest of this session**. Never written to disk, never carried into the next session. |
| **Deny with note…** | App only | Denies, and passes along what you typed — *why*, or *what to do instead*. |
| **Answer…** | App only | Picks an option for a multiple-choice question. |
| **View full plan** | App, plus **View plan** on the island | Opens the plan to read before deciding. |
| **Open on Mac** | App only | Answers nothing — it brings that session's terminal window to the front on your Mac. Needs a [local network](/sessions/settings#local-network) link. |

**Always allow** and **Deny with note** live under **More…** in the menu, and
need plugin **1.2.0** or later. **Answer…** needs **1.3.0**. If your plugin is
older, those options simply aren't offered — Nomo won't show a button the Mac
can't honour. The drawer tells you when an update is available.

## Answering a question

When the agent asks a multiple-choice question rather than for permission, the
row reads `Needs an answer` and the island says so without offering Allow or
Deny — those verbs are meaningless for a picker.

Choose **Answer…** and you get the question with its options as rows, each with
whatever explanation the agent wrote for it. Tapping a row only selects it —
nothing is sent until you hit **Submit**. **Deny** sits alongside, for when none
of the options is the answer.

Where the agent asked more than one question you'll get them one at a time, with
`Question 1 of 3` across the top and **Back** to revisit an earlier one with your
picks intact. `Select all that apply` appears where more than one answer is
allowed.

## Reviewing a plan

A session that's finished planning shows `Plan ready`. Open **View plan** and
you get the whole thing typeset, scrollable, with **Allow** and **Deny** pinned
to the bottom.

Approving from the phone is a plain approval — the same one you'd give by
picking the default at your Mac. It doesn't let you choose between accepting
edits automatically and confirming each one; that stays a terminal decision.

<Note>
  If the plan was too long to fit in one encrypted update, the sheet says so and
  tells you how much was left out. The whole plan is always still in your
  terminal. When your phone can reach the Mac directly over your local network,
  Nomo re-fetches the full text instead.
</Note>

Codex plans are **read-only** on the phone — you read them here and choose in
the terminal. [Why](/sessions/codex#plans-are-read-only).

## After you tap

The row shows `Sending…`, then settles. Your answer is sealed once on your
phone and sent to your Mac two ways at once — through the relay, and directly
over your network when that's available. Whichever arrives first wins; the
second is discarded, so a duplicate can't double-answer anything.

Nomo also verifies afterwards. A plain "OK" from the relay isn't trusted on its
own — the phone checks the prompt actually got settled, and re-sends if it
didn't. If it genuinely can't confirm, the buttons come back rather than leaving
you believing something happened.

### What the caption tells you

| Caption | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `Allowed` · `Denied` · `Always allowed` · `Answered` | It landed. |
| `Sending…` | On its way. |
| `Reconnecting…` | A retry is in progress. Transient — wait for it. |
| `Already answered on your computer` | You beat yourself to it at the Mac. |
| `Expired — continue on your computer` | The agent gave up waiting before your answer arrived. |
| `Replaced by a newer request` | The agent moved on and is asking something else now. |
| `Approvals paused — answer on your computer` | Paused on that computer — see below. |
| `Not sent — try again` | It failed and the retries ran out. Tap again. |
| `Too long to send — answer on your computer` | Your answer doesn't fit in one encrypted update. This one won't succeed on a retry. |

An answer that arrives too late is discarded, never applied. You'll never come
back to find something ran because you tapped it twenty minutes ago.

<Tip>
  Buttons need to have been on screen for about a second before a tap counts.
  If the island expands under a thumb that was already moving, that tap is
  dropped on purpose — it was never a decision.
</Tip>

## Nothing hangs forever

If your phone never answers, the prompt eventually falls back to the terminal on
its own. So does anything Nomo can't reach the phone about: no pairing, no
network, a timeout, an unreadable response — every failure path ends with the
ordinary dialog appearing at your Mac.

Some prompts never reach your phone at all, by design: sessions started by
sub-agents, and sessions running in a non-interactive permission mode where the
agent wasn't going to ask you anyway.

## Pausing it

Two switches, at different scopes.

**On your phone, for everything** — Settings → Sessions → **Answer on iPhone**.
Off means every prompt is decided at the Mac.

**On one computer** — [`/nomo-cc:approvals off`](/sessions/commands#approvals),
run on that machine. The escape hatch for when your phone is the thing that's
unavailable: prompts fall straight back to the terminal with no waiting.

Neither one unpairs anything. You'll still see sessions either way — you just
won't be asked to answer for them, and a paused computer says so at the top of
its drawer.
